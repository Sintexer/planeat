Design Schema · Meal Planning App

Visual Design Schema

Color, type, and iconography guidelines for a warm, food-forward meal planning app, across iOS, Android, and web, with full light and dark mode support.

1. Theme & Mood

The app should feel like a well-lit kitchen counter, not a spreadsheet: warm, tactile, appetite-forward. The palette is built on a warm neutral base (cream, not white; espresso, not black) with a single terracotta accent doing almost all of the color work, and a muted berry red held in reserve for a small set of secondary moments. This keeps focus on food photography, which supplies the app's real color and visual interest, while the UI itself stays quiet and confident.

Avoid a "nutrition app" clinical feel (stark white, clinical blue/green) and avoid a "delivery app" loud feel (saturated red/orange everywhere, badges, urgency cues). This is a planning tool used daily, often while cooking — it should recede and let recipe photos and text lead.

2. Color Palette

All colors are defined in OKLCH so hue, chroma, and lightness stay consistent and predictable across the whole system — hex values below are for direct implementation. Both accent colors share the same chroma (0.15) and sit in the warm half of the wheel; only hue shifts, which is what keeps them feeling like one family rather than two unrelated brand colors.

3. How Much Color to Use

Target roughly 90% neutral, 8% primary accent, 2% everything else (secondary accent + semantic colors combined) across any given screen. In practice: the background, surfaces, borders, and body text are neutral cream/espresso tones; the terracotta primary appears on one or two elements per screen at most (the primary CTA, the active tab/nav indicator, a selected state); the berry secondary and semantic colors appear only when their specific condition is true, never as ambient decoration.

This is intentionally accent-scarce. A meal planning app is opened repeatedly, often several times a day — a busy, saturated UI fatigues fast under that frequency. Scarcity also makes the accent meaningful: because terracotta appears almost nowhere else, when it does appear it reads unambiguously as "the thing to act on." Food photography is the actual color event on every screen; the UI's job is to frame it, not compete with it.

Never use the primary or secondary accent as a full-bleed background behind text (fails contrast at scale and reads as decoration, not signal). Both are used as: text/icon fills, thin borders or underlines, small fills (buttons, chips, dots, progress bars), or at low opacity (8–12%) as a tinted background behind an icon or chip — never as a large solid color field.

5. Dark Mode

Dark mode is not an inverted light mode — it uses its own warm espresso base (#17100c background, #261d19 surface) rather than pure black, so photography and warm accents don't look cold against it. The primary accent is lightened and slightly desaturated in dark mode (#e67d58 instead of #cf6139) to hold the same perceived vibrancy and stay above 4.5:1 contrast on the dark background; the same logic applies to secondary and semantic colors — lighten, don't just reuse the light-mode value. Elevation in dark mode is shown by lightening the surface a step (background → surface → raised surface), not by adding shadow, which reads poorly on dark backgrounds.

6. Typography

Headings — Sora (Google Fonts, weights 500/600/700). A geometric sans with a bit of warmth in its curves, used for screen titles, recipe names, and section headers. Body — Inter (Google Fonts, weights 400/500/600), for everything else: ingredient lists, instructions, labels, buttons, nav. Both are free, variable, and have full Latin + extended character support for localization.

Two families only. Don't introduce a third for numbers or data — use Inter's tabular figures for calorie counts, macros, and quantities so they align in tables and lists.

Role Font Weight Size
Screen title Sora 700 28–32px

Recipe/card title Sora 600 16px

Label / caption / meta Inter 500 12–13px

Minimum body text size is 15px for mobile, given how often this app is read at arm's length while cooking. Line height 1.5–1.6 for body copy.

7. Iconography

Use Phosphor Icons, Regular weight by default, Fill/Bold weight for selected or active states (nav, filled heart, checked states). Phosphor's line weight is close to Inter's and it has broad coverage of food/kitchen-adjacent glyphs (bowl, cooking pot, carrot, timer, shopping bag, calendar) without needing custom icons for most flows. Keep icons single-color — either current text color or the primary accent when indicating an active/selected state — never multi-color or filled with photography.

Standard sizes: 20px for inline/list icons, 24px for nav and toolbar icons, 32px for empty-state or onboarding illustration-adjacent icons. Stroke width 1.5px at 20–24px sizes.

8. Surfaces, Elevation & Imagery

Use elevation sparingly: a card sits on the background with either a 1px border (#e4dcd6 light / #3a312c dark) or a soft shadow (0 1px 3px rgba(23,16,12,0.08)) — not both. Reserve heavier shadow (0 8px 24px rgba(23,16,12,0.12)) for genuinely floating elements: modals, action sheets, the sticky "generate grocery list" bar. Corner radius: 12px for cards and images, 8px for buttons and inputs, full-round for chips and avatar-style elements — consistent across the app.

Recipe and ingredient photography should be warm, natural-light food photography — no illustration, no 3D render style, no stock-photo gloss. Where real photography isn't available yet, use a subtly striped neutral placeholder (not a colored block) labeled in monospace with what belongs there, e.g. "recipe photo — 4:3." Photography, not UI color, should be the most saturated thing on any given screen.

9. Accessibility

WCAG AA throughout: body text and icons at 4.5:1 minimum against their background; large text (24px+/18px+ bold) and the primary accent used decoratively (progress bar fills, dividers) at 3:1 minimum. All the text/background pairings in the token table above meet this in both light and dark mode. Never convey status by color alone — pair success/warning/error with an icon or label (a checkmark, not just a green dot).

Touch targets are 44×44px minimum on mobile, including checkboxes on grocery-list items and stepper controls on serving-size/quantity fields.

10. Implementation Notes

  • Define all colors as design tokens (semantic names — color-bg, color-primary, etc.), never hardcoded hex in components, so light/dark switch by swapping the token set.
  • Store the source-of-truth values in OKLCH; generate hex/sRGB fallbacks at build time for platforms without OKLCH support.
  • Respect system-level light/dark preference by default, with a manual override in Settings.
  • Load Sora and Inter as variable fonts where the platform supports it (web, Android) to avoid shipping multiple static weights; iOS can bundle static weights actually used (400/500/600/700).
  • Import Phosphor as an SVG icon set (not the full icon font) and tree-shake to only the glyphs used, to keep bundle size down.
